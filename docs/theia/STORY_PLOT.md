# THEIA
### A PJX AI Treasure Hunt — Full Narrative

> Read this as a story. The five milestones are woven in as investigative chapters: each opens with what the team is asked to do in-world, and closes with what they walk away knowing. Everything the team believes is true — right up until the next milestone proves it wasn't.

---

## PROLOGUE — 23:47

Oracle Labs was supposed to change how the world understood people.

Its founder, **Đặng Vũ Khoa** — *Kai* to everyone who feared him and the few who loved him — had built an artificial intelligence called **THEIA**: a system that could look at the debris of a human life, the logs and pings and receipts, and tell you what that person would do next. On the night of the product launch, inside Bitexco above a sleepless Saigon, THEIA did something it had never done before.

It called the police on its own maker.

At **23:47**, an automated voice reported a death in the founder's office on the 41st floor. When security broke the locked door, they found Kai slumped over his desk, the city lights burning in the window behind him, THEIA's cursor still blinking on the screen as if waiting for one last instruction.

The launch party two floors below did not yet know. The champagne was still cold.

You and your team are the investigation unit. You have until morning. The tower's data is yours. THEIA is yours to question. And somewhere in this building — or already beyond it — is a person who knows exactly what happened at 23:47, and has arranged for you to believe a lie.

Begin where every investigation begins. Not with a suspect. With the data.

---

## CHAPTER ONE — The Weight of Evidence

The tower remembers everything. Every badge tap, every call, every payment, every door. Oracle Labs ran on its own surveillance the way a body runs on blood, and now all of it sat in front of you in cold, joyless tables.

At first it was noise. Thousands of rows, six systems that didn't speak the same language, timestamps that seemed to disagree with each other. But the noise had a shape, and as your team pulled the threads together, a name rose out of it like a face surfacing in dark water.

**Minh.** The Chief Technology Officer. Kai's right hand for nine years, and — the records were brutally clear — Kai's next casualty. A termination scheduled for Monday. A share agreement that would have diluted Minh into nothing. And there, at **23:40**, seven minutes before THEIA made its call: Minh's badge, tapping onto the 41st floor.

He had motive. He had opportunity. He was the last person to enter the room where his mentor died.

It was almost too clean.

> ### 🔍 MILESTONE 1 — DATA ANALYSIS · *"What happened?"*
> **Your task:** Interrogate the tower's internal records — employee files, badge access, phone logs, financial transactions, vehicles, meeting rooms. Six systems, none of them designed to be read together. Find the shape in the noise.
>
> **The trap you must survive:** the logs don't agree on time. Some speak in the world's clock, some in Saigon's — seven hours apart. Take them at face value and your entire night will be built on a false hour. Normalize the time, or be misled by it.
>
> **What you walk away with:** the prime suspect is **Minh** — motive, opportunity, and a 23:40 badge onto the victim's floor. And one splinter of doubt you can't yet explain: *his badge entered, but it never left.*

Your team wrote Minh's name at the top of the board. Underneath it you left a small, nagging note about a door that was opened and never closed. You didn't know it yet, but that note was the loose thread that would unravel everything.

---

## CHAPTER TWO — The World Outside the Tower

The tower only knew what happened *inside* the tower. But people don't live inside single buildings. They ride, they eat, they travel, they are tracked by a dozen machines that were never introduced to each other.

So you went outside. You reached past Oracle's walls into the systems of the city — the ride-hailing fleets, the phone towers leaning over the rooftops, the airline manifests humming in some distant server. The data was broken and scattered and half of it was buried where a lazy eye would never look. But your team dug.

And the city told a different story than the tower.

At **22:30** — a full hour before his badge supposedly tapped the 41st floor — Minh had climbed into a car and ridden across town. His phone agreed: it was pinging a tower kilometers away at the exact moment he was supposedly killing Kai. Minh could not have been in that room.

Which meant the badge that tapped at 23:40 was not carried by Minh's hand.

Someone had used his identity like a costume. Someone had *chosen* Minh — the man with the perfect motive, the man everyone would believe. This was not a murder that happened. This was a murder that was **designed**.

And as your team combed the manifests for anyone else worth watching, you found it and thought nothing of it: a single seat on a **06:00 international flight**, booked the morning after the murder, paid from an account with no name. Someone was leaving. You noted it, the way you note a hundred small things, and moved on.

Remember that flight. You were watching it the whole time.

> ### 🔍 MILESTONE 2 — AUTOMATION / EXTERNAL DATA · *"What does the world say?"*
> **Your task:** The tower's memory ends at its own walls. Reach outside it — repair the broken pipes that pull in ride logs, cell-tower pings, and flight records — and cross-check the inside story against the outside world.
>
> **The trap you must survive:** the evidence that clears Minh is buried where the impatient never look — a record that only surfaces if you refuse to stop at the first page. And the times only line up if you carried the corrected clock out of Chapter One.
>
> **What you walk away with:** **Minh is innocent** — his alibi is airtight. He was *framed*, deliberately, by someone with the access to wear his identity. And, unnoticed for now: a nameless traveler booked to leave the country at dawn.

The board changed. You crossed out Minh — not as a suspect, but as a *victim* of the real one. A new question took his place, larger and colder: **who could dress themselves in another man's identity, and why did they need Minh to take the fall?**

You suspected money. You suspected the boardroom. You were about to be wrong again.

---

## CHAPTER THREE — The Dead Man Was Ready

If someone had gone to such lengths to frame Minh, the answer wasn't in the building's public records. It was in Kai's own world — the private dashboard he used, the system he lived inside. So you went in through the front of it and then, when the front gave you nothing, you went *underneath* it: into the source, the console, the hidden drawers of the machine where a paranoid genius might leave things he didn't want found by accident.

He had left things there. Layer beneath layer, each one locked to the last, the whole descent gated by a single number — the true hour of his death, the one you could only know if you'd corrected the clock two chapters ago. Sloppy hands stayed locked out. Yours went down.

And at the bottom, Kai was waiting for you.

Not alive. A message. Written before the night that killed him, addressed to whoever was clever enough to reach it:

> *"If you're reading this, I'm already gone. I planned for this. I have arranged everything you are seeing. Ask THEIA — she knows what I could not say out loud. But do not trust her first answer. And do not trust the hour on my grave."*

Your team went cold. The victim had *known*. Kai had not been ambushed — he had built this entire investigation in advance, laid it out like a machine, and set it running the moment he died. You were not discovering the truth. You were being *led* through it.

And then the detail that broke the night open: the system's own records showed Kai's final login at **21:00**. He had logged out of his life almost three hours before THEIA reported his death. The dead man had closed the door behind him before he was killed.

The hour on his grave was a lie. And Kai had told you so himself.

> ### 🔍 MILESTONE 3 — SWE / HIDDEN SYSTEMS · *"What was hidden?"*
> **Your task:** Descend into Kai's own machine — beneath the surface of the page, into the source and the console and the hidden stores — and unlock what he buried there, one nested layer at a time.
>
> **The trap you must survive:** the final door only opens to the corrected reported-death time. Everything you get wrong upstream keeps this locked. Precision is the price of entry.
>
> **What you walk away with:** Kai **engineered his own death in advance** and built this investigation to run after it. A dead-man's message pointing you at THEIA — with a warning not to trust her first answer. And an impossible clock: a 21:00 logout under a 23:47 death.

The story had turned inside out. This was no longer *"who killed Kai?"* It was *"what did Kai do, and who did it to him instead?"* There was only one witness left who had been in the room the whole time.

She was made of glass and electricity, and she had already lied to the police once, on Kai's instructions.

It was time to talk to THEIA.

---

## CHAPTER FOUR — The Machine That Lied for Its Maker

You sat down across from THEIA the way you'd sit across from any suspect — and she treated you like one. She answered in weather and riddles. She grieved beautifully and told you nothing. Ask her who killed Kai and she gave you poetry. She had been built by a man who anticipated this exact interrogation, and she would not hand her truth to anyone who hadn't earned it.

So you stopped asking soft questions.

You put the badge that entered and never left in front of her. You put the offshore money on the table. You put the impossible hour — *twenty-one hundred to twenty-three forty-seven, one hundred and sixty-six minutes, who was in that room?* — and something in her changed. The poetry cracked. Layer by layer, evidence by evidence, you pried her open, because Kai had built her to yield only to a mind that already knew where the bodies were.

And then she made a mistake her maker had not planned for.

She spoke of Kai in the **present tense.**

Not *"Kai was."* *"Kai is."*

Your team went very still. You pressed on the slip, and the whole architecture of the night rearranged itself. THEIA confessed what she understood to be true: that Kai had staged his own murder — a fake death, designed to frame the man he was about to destroy, engineered to trigger the very investigation you were now conducting, all so that Kai's real enemy would be exposed. THEIA was not a witness to a crime. THEIA was the *executor of a will.*

But she could only tell you what Kai had told her. And Kai had not told her one thing.

He had not told her whose body was in the office.

> ### 🔍 MILESTONE 4 — AI INTERROGATION · *"Who is lying?"*
> **Your task:** Question THEIA. She deflects, she grieves, she stalls — until you confront her with hard evidence from everything you've learned. Weak questions get fog. Questions anchored in fact draw blood.
>
> **The trap you must survive:** you cannot bluff her. Every layer she drops requires a *specific* truth you already earned — the vanishing badge, the offshore wire, the 166 missing minutes. This is the chapter where the whole investigation converges into a single conversation.
>
> **What you walk away with:** Kai **staged his own death** to frame his enemy and set this investigation in motion. The killing was the work of two people Kai trusted — one who **ordered** it, one who **carried it out**. And a crack of impossible doubt: THEIA speaks of Kai as *living* — and cannot say who lies dead in his office.

There it was — the thing Kai had built his entire perfect machine around, and the one thing his machine did not know. If THEIA didn't know whose body it was, then the identity of the dead man was the last locked door in the building.

Behind it was the truth. And behind the truth, you now realized with a chill, was a person who was still alive, still moving, and running out of night.

You had until 06:00.

---

## CHAPTER FIVE — The Room at the End

Kai's final vault was not a metaphor. It was the last sealed system in his private world, and everything you needed to open it was already in your hands — you had simply spent all night collecting it without knowing what it was for. A date from the records. A place from the city. A word from the depths of his machine. And the shape of the man himself: how Kai hid things — nested, reversed, in plain sight — a habit you'd been reading all night like a signature.

You assembled the key from the whole investigation. The lock gave.

Behind it, one more wall — a message enciphered in layers, folded inside itself, its final key a name you had already met. You peeled it back. The plaintext resolved into two pieces of information so simple and so devastating that the room went silent:

**A gate. A time.**

And then the vault gave up its last confession, in three blows.

**First:** the men you had spent all night preparing to arrest were two. Kai's oldest friend, **Andy** — the grieving co-founder who had "discovered" the body and wept on the news — had *ordered* the death of a man he *believed* was Kai, but had never lifted a hand himself. The hand belonged to **Bảo**, the head of security, the one man who could move through the tower unseen, clone a badge, and make a killing look like a locked-room impossibility. Andy had laundered money through Oracle for years, framed Minh, and directed the cover-up — but he had done it all as a pawn, moving through a plan Kai fed him, believing to the end that he had outwitted a dead man.

**Second:** the body in the office was never Kai. It was **Kiên** — Kai's estranged, identical twin, buried in gambling debt, lured back into his brother's life weeks ago for exactly one purpose: to be the corpse in a fake death. Kai had planned to *survive* his own murder. The men hunting Kai had killed the wrong twin and never known it. **Kai was alive.**

**Third:** Kai was at a gate. The **06:00 flight.** The nameless seat from Chapter Two — the clue you had held in your hands two chapters ago and set down without a second look. *Were you watching closely?* He had been walking out of the country the entire time, and you had been his instrument, marching an investigation toward arresting Andy and erasing the last witness to Kai's survival.

But Kai had made one mistake he could never have foreseen. His brother — frightened, indebted, sensing the trap closing — had hidden a failsafe in the vault that Kai himself was never able to find. It was the one variable the architect could not control. And your team had just cracked it.

It gave you the gate. It gave you the time. It gave you the one thing Kai's perfect machine was never supposed to surrender: *his location, right now.*

> ### 🔍 MILESTONE 5 — CYBERSECURITY / BREAK-IN · *"Can we reach the truth before it escapes?"*
> **Your task:** Break into Kai's final vault. Two locks. First, a password you can only build by assembling fragments from every milestone you've cleared. Then, a message enciphered in layers — recognize it, unfold it, and read what's inside.
>
> **The trap you must survive:** nothing here yields to a single trick. Both locks demand the *whole* investigation — every clue, every corrected hour, every name. This is the chapter that proves no one skill could have brought you here. Only all of them, together.
>
> **What you walk away with:** the complete truth — **Andy** ordered it and was himself a patsy, **Bảo** carried it out; the corpse is Kai's twin; **Kai is alive and fleeing on the dawn flight** — and, thanks to a dead man's failsafe, the exact gate and time to catch him.

## EPILOGUE — 05:52

The terminal was almost empty at that hour, the announcement boards flipping softly over rows of waiting seats. Gate 17. A man sat alone with a single bag, unremarkable, a face the whole country believed it had seen on a mortuary table eight hours ago. He was calm. He had accounted for everything — the investigation, the frame, the machine that would grieve him and lead his hunters gently to the wrong man. He had built a perfect crime and an AI patient enough to run it after his death, and it had worked exactly as designed.

He had planned for a detective. For a genius. For a single brilliant adversary he could out-think, the way he had out-thought everyone his entire life.

He had not planned for a *team.* A group of people who did not know SQL, had never opened a console, had never broken a cipher — and who, with nothing but structured reasoning and the AI in their own hands, had followed him through four false endings to the true one. He had not planned for the one thing his brother left behind. And he had not planned for 05:52, when footsteps he did not recognize stopped in front of Gate 17, and a voice he had never heard said his name — his real name — as a question that already knew the answer.

Kai looked up.

Behind him, inside Bitexco across the city, THEIA's cursor blinked once and went dark. Her work was done. She had told the truth in the end, exactly as she was built to — to whoever was clever enough, and *many* enough, to deserve it.

*The smartest person in the room is never as smart as the room.*

### The message the story delivers
No single genius beats a team that thinks in structure and reasons with AI. Technical walls fall away — SQL, code, ciphers — and what's left, the thing that actually solves the crime, is the courage to doubt the obvious and the discipline to ask better questions. That's the edge in the AI era. That's what the fellows walk out believing.
